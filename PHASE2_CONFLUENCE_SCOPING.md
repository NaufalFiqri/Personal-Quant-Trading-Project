# Phase 2: Multi-Condition Confluence Strategy - Scoping Proposal

Scoping only, per candidate #2 in `PHASE2_SIGNAL_CANDIDATES.md`. No code written. For review/approval before implementation starts - same discipline as `PHASE2_VOLUME_BREAKOUT_SCOPING.md` and `PHASE2_REGIME_FILTER_SCOPING.md`.

## 0. What's already on the table when this scoping starts

Two relevant results already exist, and this proposal is written to build on both rather than duplicate either:

- **Candidate #3 (regime filter), validated:** gating `maCrossoverStrategy`'s entries behind a 200-period-MA trend filter (`regime.js`'s `calculateRegime` + `strategy.js`'s `applyRegimeFilter`) reliably cut drawdown on 7/7 tickers but had a mixed, ticker-dependent effect on Sharpe/return (3/7 better, 4/7 worse). `applyRegimeFilter` was deliberately built generic - "works on any signal list, not just `maCrossoverStrategy`'s" (its own doc comment) - specifically so it could gate a different strategy later without new code.
- **Candidate #1 (volume breakout) + LEARNINGS.md lesson #10, just verified this session:** `volumeBreakoutStrategy` alone (rolling-high breakout AND volume confirmation, itself already a 2-condition AND) beat buy & hold in only 7/21 walk-forward windows, all of them in a negative-B&H window, and only beat a seeded random-entry baseline in 11/21 head-to-head windows under identical risk management - essentially a coin flip. No demonstrated edge from the entry logic alone.

That second result changes what "confluence" needs to prove here. It's not enough to ask "does adding a third condition produce a strategy that beats buy & hold" - lesson #10 already shows the 2-condition version doesn't reliably do that. The real question this candidate needs to answer is **does adding a long-period trend filter on top of an entry trigger that, alone, showed no edge over random timing, produce something that finally does** - and the test plan in §7 is built around answering that specific question, not a vaguer "is confluence good" one.

## 1. Concrete confluence definition

**Recommended: three conditions, all reused from existing code, zero new indicator functions.**

1. **Long-period trend filter:** `calculateRegime(bars, { maPeriod: 200 })` from `regime.js`, unchanged. `favorable = close > 200-period SMA`.
2. **Shorter-term entry trigger:** the breakout condition already inside `volumeBreakoutStrategy` - `close[i] > rollingMax(high, 20)[i-1]`.
3. **Volume confirmation:** also already inside `volumeBreakoutStrategy` - `volume[i] > 1.5 * rollingAvgVolume(20)[i]`.

Concretely, this is `applyRegimeFilter(volumeBreakoutStrategy(bars, params), regime)` - the exact composition `applyRegimeFilter` was built generic enough to support. Conditions 2 and 3 are already AND'd together inside `volumeBreakoutStrategy` (candidate #1); condition 1 stacks on top as a third, independent gate via the already-built, already-unit-tested regime filter. No new strategy logic, no new indicator - this is pure composition of two already-validated pieces.

**Why this combination and not the "trend filter + RSI + volume" example from `PHASE2_SIGNAL_CANDIDATES.md`, or "trend filter + MA crossover":**
- Trend filter + MA-crossover-trigger is **not a new experiment** - that's exactly what candidate #3 already tested (`applyRegimeFilter` wrapping `maCrossoverStrategy`). Reusing it here would just be re-running an already-answered question under a different name.
- Trend filter + RSI-trigger is addressed on its own in §2 below and excluded from the primary design for a specific, reasoned risk.
- Trend filter + breakout + volume is the one combination that's both genuinely untested and directly answers the open question from §0: does gating an entry trigger that showed no edge over random (breakout+volume) behind a filter that reliably reduces drawdown (trend) produce something better than either piece alone, or than random-in-the-same-regime? That's a real, previously-unanswered confluence question, not a rehash.

**Parameters:** reuse the already-validated defaults from each source unchanged - `maPeriod: 200` (regime.js), `breakoutPeriod: 20, volumePeriod: 20, volumeMultiplier: 1.5, exitPeriod: 10` (volume breakout scoping). Not re-deriving these from scratch; they're each individually validated already, and changing them here would confound "does confluence help" with "did retuning the sub-components help," which is exactly the kind of two-variables-at-once mistake lesson #4 warns against.

## 2. The RSI question, addressed directly

`PHASE2_SIGNAL_CANDIDATES.md`'s own example for this candidate names RSI as a plausible entry trigger ("a long-period trend filter... plus an RSI entry trigger plus a volume check"). It's excluded from the **primary** design above, for a specific, reasoned risk - but it's worth working through rather than dismissing by precedent alone, since "confluence" could plausibly change the calculus.

**The risk, restated from lessons #7/#8:** RSI mean-reversion's edge depends on a trade having room to draw down before it reverts. Every hard exit tested against it - percent stop (5/8/10%), default-parameter ATR stop, even a time-based cap on two of three tickers - cut into that room and hurt risk-adjusted return. The mechanism (lesson #7's addendum) was concrete and per-trade: AAPL's winning reversion trades all needed a deeper drawdown than any tested stop width allowed.

**Does requiring trend confirmation change that mechanism? A real hypothesis, not yet evidence:** gating RSI's oversold-crossup signal behind `favorable: true` (price above its own 200-day trend) changes *which* RSI signals fire - only pullbacks inside an established uptrend, not capitulation bottoms inside a downtrend. A shallow pullback with the broader trend still intact is a structurally different trade than a bottom-fishing reversal against the trend, and it's plausible (not proven) that it needs less adverse-excursion room before working, which would mean a hard stop fights it less than it fought unfiltered RSI in lesson #7. That's a reasonable story. It is also exactly the kind of plausible-sounding, untested mechanism this project has been burned by before (lesson #3's AAPL EMA standout, the original ATR-lookback and random-entry claims flagged in lesson #10's process note) - so it does not get treated as a reason to skip verification, it gets treated as a hypothesis that has to survive the same per-trade MAE-vs-stop-width analysis lesson #7's addendum already used once.

**Decision: excluded from the primary confluence design in §1, with a specific, narrow follow-up left open rather than closed off.** If a trend-gated-RSI variant is built later, it must be validated with lesson #7's addendum method applied fresh (per-trade MAE against each candidate stop width, on the *trend-filtered* trade set specifically - not assumed to inherit either the original unfiltered-RSI failure or the "confluence changes it" hypothesis without its own check). Until that's done, the breakout+volume+trend combination in §1 is the one to build and test first - it doesn't carry this open question at all.

## 3. Entry and exit logic

**Entry: AND across all three conditions**, same pattern as `volumeBreakoutStrategy`'s own internal AND (candidate #1) - not not-in-position alone, not any single condition alone. BUY only when: not already in a position, AND `favorable(bar) === true` (trend filter, from `calculateRegime`), AND `close[i] > rollingMax(high, 20)[i-1]` (breakout), AND `volume[i] > 1.5 * rollingAvgVolume(20)[i]` (volume). Mechanically this falls straight out of the composition in §1 - `applyRegimeFilter` already drops any BUY signal landing on a `favorable !== true` bar (including `null` warm-up bars, per its existing rule of treating unknown regime the same as unfavorable), so nothing new has to be written to get AND semantics; it's inherited from how the two pieces already behave.

**Exit: unchanged from `volumeBreakoutStrategy` - the same Turtle-style `close[i] < rollingMin(low, 10)[i-1]` channel breakdown, and this is a deliberate reuse, not a default.** Two reasons, both carried over from precedent already established in this project rather than re-derived from nothing:
- `applyRegimeFilter`'s own established behavior (used unchanged in candidate #3, and unchanged here) is entry-gate-only - it never touches SELL signals or force-closes an open position on a regime flip. The trend filter answers "should a *new* position be opened here," not "should an *open* position be closed here." Using the 200-period MA as an exit trigger would also be a bad fit on its own terms: it's a slow, heavily-lagging line, and exiting only when price crosses back below it would give back a large share of any gain before firing - the same "too slow" reasoning that ruled out force-close-on-regime-flip as the *entry*-side design in candidate #3 §3 applies at least as strongly to using it as an *exit*.
- Adding the trend filter doesn't change the entered trade's own shape. A confluence entry is still, at the moment of entry, a volume-confirmed breakout - the trend filter changes *which* breakouts are allowed to fire, not what a breakout trade needs once it's on. Candidate #1's original exit reasoning (a failed breakout - price breaking a level then falling back below it - is itself a meaningful failure signal worth exiting on quickly, not noise to sit through, unlike RSI's reversion trades) still applies unchanged to every trade this strategy takes, since every one of them is still, structurally, a breakout trade.

**Risk-management layer: same as every other strategy, `risk.js`'s stop-loss/circuit-breaker/sizing running underneath via `backtest.js`, unchanged.** `riskConfig: { stopLossMethod: "atr" }` is proposed as the default here for the same reason candidate #1 proposed it (breakouts often coincide with a volatility expansion a fixed percent doesn't account for) - and unlike candidate #1's original scoping, the ATR warm-up gap flagged there (`backtest.js` computing ATR only from the window-trimmed `bars`, never the lookback-extended fetch) is already fixed as of this session (`warmupBars` parameter, see `LEARNINGS.md` lesson #10's process note) - `lookbackMonths` set in §4 below will warm up the ATR stop automatically, with no separate workaround needed this time.

## 4. Lookback / warm-up

- Trend filter needs 200 prior bars of `close` history - by a wide margin the binding constraint, same as candidate #3.
- Breakout needs 20 prior bars of `high`; volume confirmation needs 20 prior bars of `volume`; exit needs 10 prior bars of `low` - all far shorter than the trend filter's requirement, same relationship candidate #1 already had between its own conditions.
- **Combined warm-up bottleneck: 200 trading days**, set entirely by the trend filter, identical to candidate #3's finding (the breakout/volume/exit periods don't move this number at all - they're all well under it).

**Proposed `lookbackMonths: 11`**, reusing candidate #3's exact value and reasoning (~231 trading days at ~21 trading days/month, comfortable margin over the 200 needed) rather than re-deriving a new number - the binding constraint is identical, so the same fix applies unchanged.

**Window length: 18 months**, reusing candidate #3's reconsidered value (once lookback decouples warm-up from window length, a shorter window yields more independent samples across a fixed historical range) and matching candidate #1's own window length exactly - this also means the walk-forward call here can use the identical `startDate`/`endDate`/`windowMonths`/`minWindowMonths` already used for both prior validations, which is what makes the direct three-way comparison in §7 possible without re-deriving window boundaries.

## 5. File structure

**No new strategy function needed in `strategy.js`.** Per §1, this is `applyRegimeFilter(volumeBreakoutStrategy(bars, params), regime)`, both already exported. The only candidate for new code is a small, optional convenience wrapper (e.g. `confluenceBreakoutStrategy(bars, params)` that internally calls `calculateRegime` + `volumeBreakoutStrategy` + `applyRegimeFilter` and returns the combined signal list) purely so call sites and `runWalkForward` don't have to repeat the three-call composition inline every time. This wrapper would contain no new decision logic of its own - it would be a call-site convenience, not a new strategy - and whether it's worth adding is a small implementation-time judgment call, not something that needs approval here.

**No new file.** Doesn't warrant its own module the way `risk.js`/`regime.js` did (neither is a strategy that emits its own signals; both are reusable utilities consumed *by* strategies) - this is closer in role to `volumeBreakoutStrategy` itself, which lives directly in `strategy.js`, and the optional wrapper above (if added) belongs there too, following the same precedent candidate #1's scoping already established.

## 6. Test plan

Same order of operations as both prior candidates, with one addition: the random-entry baseline comparison, run **two ways** rather than one, since a single random comparison here would conflate two different questions.

1. **Unit tests** (`test-confluence.js` or added to an existing suite) - hand-constructed bars, mirroring `test-regime.js`/`test-volume-breakout.js` conventions:
   - All three conditions true -> BUY fires.
   - Breakout + volume true, regime unfavorable -> BUY correctly blocked (confirms the AND includes the trend filter, not just the two conditions already tested in `test-volume-breakout.js`).
   - Breakout + volume true, regime still `null` (inside the 200-bar warm-up) -> BUY correctly blocked, same treatment as a genuine unfavorable reading (this is `applyRegimeFilter`'s existing, already-tested rule - the point of this specific case is confirming the composition inherits it correctly, not re-testing `applyRegimeFilter` itself).
   - Regime favorable, breakout true, volume not confirmed -> still blocked (confirms composition didn't accidentally weaken volume breakout's own existing AND).
   - Exit (close below the 10-day low) fires identically regardless of regime, including on a bar where regime has since flipped unfavorable (confirms the entry-gate-only behavior from §3 - an open position isn't touched by the filter).
2. **Single-ticker sanity check** (one ticker, one window) - report signal counts at each composition stage: raw `volumeBreakoutStrategy` BUY count -> count after the regime filter -> at least one concrete annotated example of a blocked signal (breakout+volume fired, but the bar was below its own 200-day trend) and one concrete kept example, same format as candidate #3's AAPL sanity check.
3. **Full 7-ticker walk-forward** (AAPL, MSFT, JNJ, GOOGL, AMZN, NVDA, META), 18-month windows, `lookbackMonths: 11`, `riskConfig: { stopLossMethod: "atr" }` - report the same Sharpe/MaxDD/WinRate/Return/Trades table (per-ticker and averaged) used for both prior candidates, plus per-ticker direction of effect explicitly (not just the average), per lesson #9's standing rule.
4. **Isolate the trend filter's own marginal effect** by comparing this run directly against the already-existing `volume-breakout-validation.js` output (same tickers, same windows, same lookback, same risk config, breakout+volume only, no trend gate) - no need to re-run that script, its committed output is the baseline. This answers "did adding the third condition help beyond what breakout+volume alone already gets," the same isolate-the-filter methodology candidate #3 used in its own §5 step 5.
5. **Random-entry baseline, run two ways - this is the check the last investigation found most informative, and it needs to be precise about what it's isolating here:**
   - **(a) Ungated random**, reusing `randomEntryStrategy` exactly as built for lesson #10 (same seed, same matched frequency), compared against the full 3-condition confluence strategy. This tests "does the whole confluence strategy beat naive random timing" - the most basic bar to clear.
   - **(b) Regime-gated random** - `applyRegimeFilter(randomEntryStrategy(bars, params), regime)`, the same random entries but restricted to bars where the trend filter is favorable, at a frequency re-matched to fire about as often as the confluence strategy does *within* favorable-regime bars specifically (not the same raw probability as (a), since favorable bars are a subset of all bars). This is the more precise test: it holds the trend-filter's own regime-restriction effect constant on both sides, isolating whether the breakout+volume entry trigger adds anything *on top of* "only trade when the trend is favorable" - directly analogous to how lesson #10 held risk management constant to isolate the entry logic's own value, applied one level up.
   - Both comparisons get reported, not just one - if the confluence strategy beats (a) but not (b), the honest read is "the edge (if any) comes from the trend filter's regime restriction, not from the breakout+volume trigger specifically," which would be a real, useful, and different conclusion than "confluence works."
6. **No-signal windows are expected to be more common here than for volume breakout alone**, same reasoning as candidate #3 §5 step 6 - three ANDed conditions is a strictly stricter gate than two. Watch for it explicitly and report it per-ticker, don't fold it into an average silently (the exact bug already fixed once in this project's history, per `LEARNINGS.md`'s data-bug/zero-trade-window entries).
